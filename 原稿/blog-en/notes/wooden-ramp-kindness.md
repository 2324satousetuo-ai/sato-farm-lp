# What a Wooden Ramp Taught Me About the Give-and-Take of Kindness — The Sato Farms Way

November 12, 2026
## 1. On My Wife's Way to Work

It happened while my wife was driving to work.

Same road. Same time.

A large truck came toward her, drifting over the center line.

Watch out!

She turned the wheel a little to the left. At that very moment—

Crash!

The tire scraped against the curb. The car suddenly sank on one side.

When she checked, there was no visible damage to the car itself. But both tires on the left side, front and rear, were flat.

## 2. I Rushed to Help, But...

My wife called me.

Changing tires? Leave it to me. I do it myself every year.

We have three cars: a Harrier, a Raum, and a Minicab. I change the tires on all of them myself. It is an easy job for me.

I packed all my tools and two winter tires into my mini truck. I was ready for anything and hurried to the scene.

But there was a big problem.

The jack would not fit.

Neither at the front nor at the rear on the left side. The car was leaning so low that there was no room to put the jack underneath.

I had the tools. I had the tires. I had the skills.

Still, I could not even get started.

I was in trouble. What should I do?

## 3. Beyond "We Can't Do That"

"If only I had a floor jack," I thought.

I called my usual car dealer.

They seemed reluctant to lend me one. Instead, they suggested calling roadside assistance or using my car insurance.

Their answer made sense. I could not blame them.

Still, that was not what I needed right then.

This was not going to work.

I also knew a local gas station. I got into my mini truck and was about to head there.

Just then, I noticed a car repair shop right nearby.

## 4. Taking a Chance

I had no time to lose. I walked in, explained the problem, and asked for help.

The mechanic asked me,

"Can the car still move a little, even with the flat tires?"

"Yes," I answered.

Then he brought out something unexpected: a wooden ramp. It was made to go under a tire so the car could drive up onto it and gain some height.

"Will this really work?"

Honestly, I had never thought of that. I had come to borrow a floor jack, not a wooden ramp.

Still not quite sure, I returned to the car and slowly drove it onto the ramp.

The car went up, and the jack fit easily!

After that, everything was surprisingly simple. I finished the job in no time.

## 5. A Thank-You to the Kind Mechanic

He had lent me the ramp so kindly. I had to thank him properly.

After finishing the work, I went back to his shop.

"Please accept this as a small thank-you."

"Oh, no. I didn't do it for money."

He tried to refuse, but I did not give up.

"I know, but please accept it. This is how I want to show my thanks."

I handed him 2,000 yen.

## 6. Everything Worked Out

He had helped me so willingly. The work went smoothly. My wife was grateful, too.

Although he ran a repair shop, he had lent his equipment to a complete stranger who had simply walked in asking for help. He did it free of charge.

I was truly grateful for his kindness.

I offered him 2,000 yen as a small token of my appreciation. In the end, everything worked out just right.

## 7. From Sato Farms

Today, I grow rice and vegetables. I run Sato Farms.

I believe farming is much the same as this story.

You cannot manage rice fields alone. Neighbors share water. We lend and borrow machines. Asking someone to give us a hand is part of everyday life.

Each time, there is an exchange of kindness.

It is not about money. It is not a duty. It is a genuine exchange of kindness between people.

The wooden ramp from that repair shop had no price tag. The 2,000 yen I gave was not just a payment, either.

I want my "Sato Rice" to be the same.

I grow rice with time and care, and deliver it with a happy heart. I want the people who eat it to receive it with the same warmth.

I hope to build a farm where these warm connections grow, one grain at a time.

When someone is in trouble, there is a person who quietly lends a hand. And we return that kindness from the heart.

That is the Sato Farms way.
