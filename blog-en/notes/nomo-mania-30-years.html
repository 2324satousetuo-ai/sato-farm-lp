30 Years as a Nomo Mania

Japanese Players Making Their Mark in MLB

I have been following Major League Baseball (MLB) since 1995, the year Hideo Nomo made his MLB debut.

To be more exact, I was interested in MLB even before that. I knew about Masanori Murakami, who became the first Japanese player to play in the Major Leagues in 1964.

Nomo's success brought MLB closer to Japanese fans. NHK BS began broadcasting more live MLB games, and passionate fans like me became true "Nomo fans."

If I had to choose the four greatest Japanese MLB players from my own point of view, I would pick Hideo Nomo, Ichiro Suzuki, Hideki Matsui, and Shohei Ohtani.

Japanese MLB Players by Generation and Role

  • Pioneer Pitchers
  • Hideo Nomo: A true pioneer who helped open the door for Japanese players in MLB with his famous "Tornado" pitching style. He also threw two no-hitters.
  • Hideki Irabu, Kazuhiro Sasaki, Shigetoshi Hasegawa, Masato Yoshii
  • Pioneer Hitters
  • Ichiro Suzuki: In his first MLB season in 2001, he won the batting title, the stolen base title, the MVP award, and the Rookie of the Year award. In 2004, he set an amazing record with 262 hits in a single season.
  • Tsuyoshi Shinjo, Hideki Matsui, Kazuo Matsui, Kenji Johjima, Tadahito Iguchi, So Taguchi
  • Leading Pitchers of the 2000s and 2010s
  • Daisuke Matsuzaka, Hiroki Kuroda, Koji Uehara, Hisashi Iwakuma, Yu Darvish, Masahiro Tanaka, Kenta Maeda
  • Today's Stars and the Next Generation
  • Shohei Ohtani: A one-of-a-kind player who continues to make baseball history as both a pitcher and a hitter.
  • Yusei Kikuchi, Seiya Suzuki, Masataka Yoshida, Kodai Senga, Shota Imanaga, Yoshinobu Yamamoto, Roki Sasaki, Kazuma Okamoto, Munetaka Murakami, Tatsuya Imai

A Personal Memory of Ichiro

Ichiro's first MLB season was 2001. He recorded 242 hits and won several major awards, including the batting title, the stolen base title, the MVP award, and Rookie of the Year.

At that time, I was staying in the United States. One day, someone at a bar in New York asked me, "Who is this guy?"

But I knew very little about him. All I knew was that he had played for the ORIX BlueWave in Japan.

I found myself wondering, too: "What kind of player is he, anyway?"

I still remember how excited people across America were as this Japanese newcomer made such an impressive start in the Major Leagues.

Looking to the Future

The path Hideo Nomo helped open has led to a new era. Today, Japanese players come to MLB and take on important roles on their teams.

Thirty years ago, it would have been hard to imagine seeing so many Japanese players competing at such a high level.

As a fan who has followed MLB for decades, I look forward to seeing what Japanese players will achieve in the years to come.
