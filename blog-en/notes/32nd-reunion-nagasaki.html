Agatsuma November 9, 2026

Our 32nd Reunion and My Journey to Visit All 47 Prefectures of Japan

On November 5, our annual reunion was held in Fukuoka.

Thirty-three years ago, we traveled around the world in one month as members of an overseas study group organized by Japan's Ministry of Education. Since then, we have continued to meet every year. This year marked our 32nd reunion.

I flew from Haneda Airport to Fukuoka Airport. From Hakata, I took a train to Kokura, where we enjoyed our usual reunion dinner and stayed overnight. Our memories of the trip 33 years ago are slowly fading, but we always have plenty to talk about.

The next day, we visited Kokura Castle. After that, we returned to Hakata and took an express bus to Nagasaki.

Nagasaki: The Last of Japan's 47 Prefectures

Nagasaki was the only one of Japan's 47 prefectures that I had never visited. So I decided to visit Nagasaki after the reunion in Fukuoka.

Nagasaki City is a beautiful port city with many hills and a rich international history. During the Edo period, Japan had limited contact with other countries, but trade with foreign countries continued through Dejima, a small trading area in Nagasaki. The city also played an important role in Japan's modernization.

Today, streetcars and buses run through the city, and many tourists visit its historic places.

I visited Peace Park, the Atomic Bomb Museum, Oura Cathedral, Chinatown, and the Dejima area. I stayed at an APA Hotel near Ohato. It was my first time staying at this hotel.

On the way home, I flew from Nagasaki to Kobe and then to Haneda Airport.

With this trip, I had finally set foot in all 47 prefectures of Japan.

However, there is one small detail. I have only passed through Saga and Kumamoto Prefectures without really staying there. Next year, I hope to spend some time in both places and complete my journey around Japan in a true sense.

I am especially looking forward to visiting Kumamoto Castle.

An Unexpected Event on the Train: A Safety Check

On my way back to Gunma from Tokyo, I was traveling on the JR Ueno-Tokyo Line when the train suddenly came to a stop.

According to the announcement, the driver had heard an unusual noise. Later, we were told that it was probably the sound of the train hitting a small animal. The train stopped for about 15 minutes.

After a while, I saw the driver get off the train and walk along the railway line to check the train and the area around it. After making sure that it was safe to continue, the train started moving again.

During this time, the conductor continued to make announcements and assist passengers.

The driver checked the situation outside, while the conductor took care of the passengers inside. I was impressed by how carefully they worked together to keep everyone safe.

It is not often that we get to see this kind of work up close. Watching them made me appreciate the professionalism of the railway staff who keep our daily train services safe.

Our Reunion in the Future and Better Access to Haneda Airport

Our reunion group originally had 22 members, but only seven people attended this year. Even so, our group leader and the person who organizes the meetings are still full of energy.

We hope to continue this special reunion for many more years. Next year's meeting is also planned for Fukuoka and Kokura.

By the way, for people living in Gunma, Haneda Airport is much easier to use than Narita Airport. However, changing trains to the Keikyu Line at Shinagawa Station can still be a little tiring.

JR East is now building a new railway line called the Haneda Airport Access Line. It will connect Tokyo Station directly with Haneda Airport.

The line is scheduled to open in fiscal year 2031. Once it is ready, the journey from Tokyo Station to Haneda Airport is expected to take only about 18 minutes.

There are also plans to provide direct train services from the Takasaki Line area, including Gunma.

This could make traveling from Gunma to Haneda Airport much easier than it is today.

I am already looking forward to its completion, as well as to many more journeys in the future.
