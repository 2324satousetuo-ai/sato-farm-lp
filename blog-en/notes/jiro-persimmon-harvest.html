After Harvesting Jiro Persimmons | Making Dried Persimmons

We finally finished harvesting our Jiro persimmons. The tree produced more fruit than we expected this year, so next year might be an "off year" with fewer persimmons.

We use our persimmons in three ways:

1. Eating fresh: We eat them every day. Having one right after a hot bath is the best!

2. Gifts: Every year, we send them to people who have helped us.

3. Dried persimmons: We spread them out on bamboo baskets to dry in the sun.

One great thing about dried persimmons is that they last a long time. At home, we keep them in a cool, dry place, and we are still eating ones from last year. They lose their nice shape a bit, but they are still very sweet. However, how well they keep depends on the environment. Be sure to check them before eating, and put them in the freezer if you want to keep them for a long time.

Persimmons: Fresh or Dried, What's the Difference?

There is an old saying in Japan: "When persimmons turn red, doctors turn pale."

Persimmons have been enjoyed for generations. They are delicious both fresh and dried in the sun, but each has its own benefits.

Fresh Persimmons

Fresh persimmons are juicy and rich in vitamin C.

They are among the fruits with a high vitamin C content. One medium-sized persimmon can provide a large amount of this vitamin.

They also contain beta-carotene and potassium, which are important nutrients for our bodies.

Dried Persimmons

As persimmons lose water during drying, fiber, potassium, and beta-carotene become more concentrated by weight.

They also become sweeter, with a rich, natural taste and no added sugar.

The white powder on the surface is usually natural sugar crystals, called kakishimo. However, if it looks fluffy or fuzzy, it may be mold, so take care.

Vitamin C decreases greatly during drying. However, dried persimmons become a sweet snack that is rich in fiber and can be stored for a longer time.

Fresh persimmons are a good source of vitamin C, while dried persimmons contain more fiber and potassium per 100 grams.

The same fruit offers different tastes and nutritional benefits, depending on how it is prepared.
