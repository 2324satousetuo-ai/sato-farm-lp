About Nakanojo

Nakanojo:
A Land of Nature and Hot Springs

Located in northwestern Gunma's Agatsuma region, Nakanojo is a town blessed with pristine nature and famous hot springs like Shima Onsen and Sawatari Onsen. Pure subterranean water from the Shima and Nakuta Rivers, significant day-and-night temperature swings, and lush forests create the perfect terroir that nurtures the rich flavor of Sato Farms' rice and vegetables.

  • Subterranean water from the Shima & Nakuda Rivers Mineral-rich water nourishes the paddies
  • Four seasons of Agatsuma Temperature swings concentrate flavor in our crops
  • Fields and paddies Koshihikari rice and seasonal vegetables
  • Hot springs & nature Renowned onsen towns including Shima and Sawatari

Rice Trends in Agatsuma District

  • Gunma Prefecture's rice varieties differ by region. Double-cropping areas in central, western, and eastern Gunma center on "Goropikari" and "Asahi no Yume," while single-cropping areas in the north and east, including Agatsuma District, center on "Koshihikari."
  • Koshihikari from the Hokumo region has earned the top "Special A" rating in Japan's national rice-tasting rankings three times (2008, 2009, and 2020 harvests).
  • In recent years, cultivation of the new variety "Niji no Kirameki" has been expanding in the prefecture, and it was added to Gunma's official regional variety list for the 2025 harvest.
  • "Niji no Kirameki" features Koshihikari-level taste, plus heat tolerance, higher yield, lodging resistance, and strong resistance to rice stripe disease.
  • Koshihikari remains dominant in the district, but heat-tolerant varieties are likely to be adopted and used alongside it in the future. July 5, 2026

Sato Farms' New Way to Order Rice

From the mountains to your table,
at its very best.

A new "grow-and-deliver" service from Sato Farms in Nakanojo, Gunma.

Introduction | Rice We're Proud to Share, Straight From Our Fields

Here in Nakanojo, a town in Gunma Prefecture, we have been growing rice for many years. Again and again, people tell us the same thing: "The rice from this area smells wonderful and tastes great."

Our region, called Hokumo, is known for a big difference in temperature between day and night, and for clean, clear water. These are perfect conditions for growing rice. Koshihikari rice from the Hokumo region has earned the top score, called "Special A," in Japan's national rice-tasting rankings three times: in 2008, 2009, and 2020. Nakanojo is also home to a well-known local rice brand called "Hanayukari." We want to bring this rich local flavor straight to your table, in the best condition this rich land can offer.

That is why Sato Farms built a new website, and along with it, a new way for farmers and customers to connect directly.

Why Buy Directly From a Farmer?

In recent years, climate change and other big changes in the world have made rice farming less predictable than ever. It has become hard for farmers to plan for the future.

That is why we decided to take the first step ourselves, starting small. Our goal is not simply to sell rice and have people buy it. We want to build something that can last for many years: a relationship where farmers and customers support each other without strain, and where, over time, we can help protect farming across our whole region.

To do this, Sato Farms' direct-sale program stands on three main ideas.

Three Things We Promise

1. A Fair Price You Can Understand

Growing rice depends on nature. Each year's weather and harvest are different. Because of this, we will share our prices in stages, as the year goes on:

  • Early in the year: A reference price (just an estimate) — brown rice 30 kg = ¥20,000
  • Before harvest: A tentative price (adjusted based on how the crop is growing)
  • After harvest: The final price (based on the actual harvest and quality)

To be honest, there is no perfect formula that can calculate the "correct" price using only numbers. In the end, we at Sato Farms have to make that judgment call ourselves. And when the amount of rice is limited, we cannot ignore customers who show strong interest in buying, or who have supported us for a long time. This doesn't mean "whoever pays the most wins." It simply means we want to respond with care to the people who reach out to us with care.

We will share our reasoning and the steps behind each price as openly as we can. This system is not perfect yet. It's a new start. As we go, we will listen to what our customers tell us, and keep improving it.

2. Steady Supply, Supported by the Whole Region

If Sato Farms alone cannot grow enough rice in a given year, we will bring in rice from trusted partner farms that follow the same growing standards as we do. We take full responsibility for the quality of that rice too. By starting with our own farm and slowly building connections with others nearby, we hope to make sure you always have delicious, reliable rice.

3. Sign Up at Your Own Pace

To avoid wasting rice and to plan supply carefully, we ask people to register for free, choosing the level that matches their interest:

  • Level 1: I definitely want to buy. (We will reserve rice for you first.)
  • Level 2: I'll decide once I see the price. (We will tell you the price as soon as it's set.)
  • Level 3: I just want updates for now. (We will keep you posted on the farm and the crops.)

Once you register, we will send updates through LINE and other tools, whenever there's something worth sharing. And no matter whether you register or not, anyone can read about how this whole system works — it's open to everyone.

Why We Recommend Brown Rice

At Sato Farms, we want you to enjoy rice at its freshest and most flavorful. That's why we recommend buying it as brown rice.

Using a small home rice-polishing machine, or a coin-operated rice mill near you, you can polish just the amount you need, right before you cook it. It takes a little extra effort, but the reward is a warm bowl of rice with the wonderful smell and taste of freshly polished rice. That small effort is more than just a task — it's a way to support your local farmer directly, and a wonderful way to enjoy your food.

Closing Thoughts | Building a Warm and Lasting Relationship

Because we work with nature, we want to build a relationship between grower and buyer that feels natural and lasts a long time — one where neither side has to strain too hard.

If this idea of buying delicious rice directly from a farm in Nakanojo speaks to you, we would be glad to have you register. Let's take this first step together, connecting your table to our fields.
